## 4. Decision criteria

The route comparison uses the following criteria without inventing a universal numeric score:

- contribution to the original multi-expert-system goal;
- genuinely new information versus repeated historical evidence;
- reuse of accepted assets and closed dependencies;
- current authority/readiness;
- implementation and calendar cost;
- risk of endless local optimization or historical overfitting;
- reversibility and fail-closed operation;
- ability to expose the next real bottleneck rather than guess it in advance.

## 5. Four-perspective review

The four perspectives were evaluated separately against the same frozen dossier. Because the OCI
AgentDock shell could not start its already-authenticated Astra runtime (`/bin/sh: permission
denied`), while the alternate Devops node lacked the required provider authentication and the other
nodes lacked Codex, this review uses a **single-assistant four-perspective simulation**, not four
independent external-model sessions. No valid external reviewer output was produced before this
fallback. The route decision therefore rests primarily on the repository evidence above; the role
views are decision support rather than independent empirical evidence.

### 5.1 Short-term / event-driven

Selects **A**. The current missing information is whether the frozen two-expert topology remains
usable when each T-close decision is committed before later outcomes are known. Adding another
expert or richer aggregator logic first would increase decision surfaces without knowing which
surface actually fails prospectively. The most informative short-term evidence is prospective
coverage, disagreement, abstention, timing behavior, and divergence between Shadow and the isolated
paper lane.

### 5.2 Fund / midterm risk

Selects **A**. The accepted position-risk expert has a large historical counterfactual effect, but
its true value is still development-only. A finite prospective observation budget and balanced
paper ledger directly test whether its risk constraints remain useful without silently changing the
strategy. Historical refinement before this step risks converting a risk-control expert into a
sample-specific optimizer.

### 5.3 Quant research

Selects **A**. The dominant epistemic gap is forward/external validity, not another historical
parameter or representation experiment. P6 produces information unavailable from another replay:
outcome-blind observations, post-activation DWD identities, target maturation, dependence-aware
scorecards, and append-only correction dispositions. More historical attribution can refine
mechanism understanding but cannot answer this question.

### 5.4 Data governance / warehouse

Selects **A**. P6 is the first route that forces the complete future evidence chain to become a
first-class audited object: activation boundary, first admissible DWD release, publication/
visibility time, effective-view identity, access ledger, late-correction disposition, immutable
forward Run, and paper-ledger lineage. Historical sector archaeology is a separate useful data
problem, but it does not block prospective operation of the already-admitted two-expert topology.

## 6. Synthesis

All four perspectives select route **A: P6 prospective closed-budget validation**.

The reason is not that the current two-expert strategy is already known to be good. The reason is
that Quanthus has reached the point where the highest-value unknown is whether the accepted expert
architecture survives **real information arrival and outcome-blind time**, including the
possibility that it performs poorly or remains inconclusive.

The selected route reuses the existing expert protocol, two-expert topology, aggregator, Worker
contracts, P2 referee, lifecycle artifacts, and candidate evidence surface. It also has an approved
architecture and an explicitly ready first gate. In contrast, routes B-G mostly add complexity or
repeat historical evidence before the present topology has faced the next epistemic boundary.

6. no new expert, Stage action mapping, aggregator mechanism, historical-sector reconstruction, P5
